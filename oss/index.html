Setting up a One-Stop Shop

Four phases, twenty-three steps. For each one: what it costs you in hours, what you can hand to somebody else, and what will be difficult whatever you do.

Most people reading this already have a full portfolio. Phase 1 exists so that this becomes part of your job rather than something you do in the evenings. If you get through Phase 1 and stop there, that is a reasonable place to stop.

The four phases
Who this is for

You already carry a full portfolio. Nothing here becomes real until somebody above you agrees it is part of your job. That agreement is the whole output of this phase.

What you will have at the end
  • A two-page note, backed by four local figures
  • Three answers to the objections you will actually get
  • One named person who is carrying this when you are not in the room
  • A line in your workplan, or a clear no — both are useful

Find three or four local figures that make the case obvious to someone who has never thought about renovation.

Start hereOpen your municipal energy or climate plan today and copy out the renovation target and its date. That is your first figure; the other three hang off it.
Why this mattersYou will be asked for a figure in the first two minutes, and asked again in every meeting after that. Finding four now means not hunting for them six separate times.
Hand off or borrowA colleague or intern can pull the numbers. Choosing which four is your job.
The realityIf the local data does not exist, use a stated estimate and say it is one. Nobody has lost a meeting for using a marked estimate. Several have lost one by arriving with nothing.

What you have when it is doneFour local figures, each with a named source you can defend.

Guidance, checkpoints and resources

Pull your figures from sources your council already trusts — national statistics, the municipal energy plan, the regional building register. Borrowed authority is worth more than precision here.

Four numbers are usually enough: housing stock by age and type; your current renovation rate against the target in your own climate plan; households in or near energy poverty; average annual household energy spend.

Write each one as a sentence a non-specialist would repeat at dinner. If you cannot say it without reading it, it is not the right number.

What others actually didSeven municipalities around Heerlen in the Netherlands co-financed their service together, budgeting €1.2 million to cover five years of development and operation. That single figure is what made the proposition legible to seven separate councils.Attributed to the INNOVATE guidebook (Energy Cities, 2020) and EU Peers Deliverable 4.4 (2026).
Checkpoint questions
  • Can you say each figure out loud, with its source, without notes?
  • Does at least one of them describe a gap between a commitment and reality?
  • Would a resident recognise the picture these numbers paint?
 
Common mistakeCollecting twenty figures. You will use four.

Four sentences that prevent your decision-maker picturing the wrong thing.

Start hereWrite four sentences now and read them aloud to one colleague who has never heard the term. If they ask a clarifying question, the sentences are not finished.
Why this mattersThree people in the room are each picturing something different — a call centre, a grant desk, a list of builders. If that is not settled in the first minute, the discussion runs on the wrong subject and you find out at the end.
Hand off or borrowNobody. You will repeat these four sentences a hundred times.
The realitySomebody will say “we already do this”. Usually they do about a fifth of it. Know which fifth, and say so without contradicting them.

What you have when it is doneFour sentences you can deliver without notes.

Guidance, checkpoints and resources

What it is: a single point of contact that carries a household from first question to finished works — advice, finance, contractors, follow-up — without handing them a list and wishing them luck.

Then build the fence. It is not a call centre, which answers questions and stops. It is not a grant desk, which processes subsidies and never touches the works. It is not a contractor directory, which transfers the problem back to the household.

Name the local thing your reader will otherwise confuse it with. Every territory has one.

What others actually didHeerlen defined its service concretely: a catalogue of energy renovation products and services with fixed prices and guaranteed quality. Reimarkt, run by KAW in the Netherlands, went further and sold standardised products aimed at different customer segments. Neither is an advice desk, and saying so early prevented the confusion.Attributed to the INNOVATE guidebook (Energy Cities, 2020) and EU Peers Deliverable 4.4 (2026).

  • Have you defined it by what it excludes, not only by what it includes?
  • Would a contractor recognise their role in your description?

Locate the commitment your council has already signed that this service would help deliver.

Start hereEmail the officer who drafted your climate or housing plan and ask which commitment a renovation service would help deliver. They will answer in a paragraph.
Why this mattersA new initiative competes with everything else on a crowded agenda. A delivery route for a commitment already signed does not — it arrives with its own justification. That difference decides whether you get twenty minutes now or a slot next year.
Hand off or borrowAsk whoever drafted the climate or housing plan. They will find the sentence in five minutes.
The realityIf your authority has no renovation commitment at all, this is a considerably longer project than this page suggests. Better to know that now.

What you have when it is doneOne quoted commitment, with document and page, and the name of whoever owns it.

Guidance, checkpoints and resources

Read the climate and energy plan, the SECAP, the housing strategy, and any regional renovation target. Find the sentence this service would help deliver and quote it back verbatim.

Where regional or national renovation targets are tied to funding, that is the strongest hook available. Follow it to whoever administers the money.

If you find nothing, that is also an answer — and it tells you the first thing to change.

A cost range for year one and a realistic view of who pays for it.

Start hereBook thirty minutes with your finance officer this week. Bring no numbers — ask them what a two-to-three-person service normally costs in your authority.
Why this mattersThe money question is the one that ends the meeting. A range with the uncertain parts marked survives scrutiny. A confident number that turns out wrong does not, and you are the one who carries it.
Hand off or borrowAsk a service elsewhere for their real figures rather than modelling from nothing. Most will share them.
The realityTwo things that catch public bodies. Municipal budgets are renegotiated annually, so multi-year commitments are rarely on offer. And where the service competes with private providers, EU state aid rules apply. Raise both yourself, before somebody else does.

What you have when it is doneA first-year cost range and a named funding mix, with uncertainties marked.

Guidance, checkpoints and resources

Cost side: two or three staff, premises, a CRM, a communications budget, travel, overheads. Give a range, not a point.

Funding side, which is the harder half: most services in Europe depend substantially on public money, particularly in the early years. Name the realistic mix — municipal budget, regional or national programme, EU funding, and eventually household fees or contractor commissions.

Mark the uncertain parts as uncertain, in writing. A finance officer trusts a stated unknown far more than a confident guess.

What others actually didTwo real anchors at opposite scales. Reimarkt reported start-up costs of €120,000 and fixed operating costs of €86,000 a year. The Brussels Capital Region service runs on roughly €500,000 a year for a team of sixteen. Your figure sits somewhere between, and saying which end is honest.Attributed to the INNOVATE guidebook (Energy Cities, 2020) and EU Peers Deliverable 4.4 (2026).
Checkpoint questions
  • What proportion of your year-one funding is public?
  • Which single line item are you least sure about?
  • Have you shown this to anyone who controls a budget?
 
Common mistakeProjecting self-financing. Real services take two to three years to break even, and five to eight years before the model is genuinely viable — the INNOVATE case studies state both plainly. Anything faster in your model reads as inexperience.

Identify who can authorise this, and prepare for the arguments that will be used against it.

Start hereWrite the three objections you expect on one sheet, then take it to the most sceptical colleague you have and ask them to add a fourth.
Why this mattersProposals rarely fail in the meeting. They fail quietly afterwards, when the agenda gets crowded and nobody senior is carrying them.
Hand off or borrowNo.
The realityPolitical cycles. If there is an election inside a year, say what happens to this afterwards — or somebody else will say it for you.

What you have when it is doneOne named sponsor, three objections, three written answers.

Guidance, checkpoints and resources

Work out who can actually authorise this, who briefs them, and who will resist. All three matter. The briefer is often more decisive than the decision-maker.

Write down the three objections you will hear. Usually some version of: we already do this, there is no money, and this is not our job.

Draft one paragraph against each. Then test them on a colleague who enjoys disagreeing with you.

What others actually didBrussels opened six neighbourhood one-stop shops and later regrouped them into a single location — described in the INNOVATE guide as a political decision, not an operational one. Political decisions override operational logic, so know who will make yours.Attributed to the INNOVATE guidebook (Energy Cities, 2020) and EU Peers Deliverable 4.4 (2026).
Checkpoint questions
  • Can you name one person who would put their reputation behind this?
  • Have you rehearsed the objections with someone hostile?
  • Which objection do you have the weakest answer to — and what would fix it?
 
Common mistakePreparing for the friendly questions.

Assemble the four figures, the mandate, the costs and the objections into two sides of paper.

Start hereDraft page one only, in one sitting, and stop. If page one does not stand alone, page two will not save it.
Why this mattersTwo pages is what actually gets read. It is also what you forward when somebody asks you about this again in six months, which they will.
Hand off or borrowNo. But have one person read it who will tell you honestly that it is dull.
The realityAsk for a mandate and some hours, not a budget line. Budget lines enter an annual cycle. Mandates can be given in a meeting.

What you have when it is doneTwo pages. One decision requested. Ideally, hours allocated in your workplan.

Guidance, checkpoints and resources

Page one: the local problem in your four figures; the commitment it delivers; what the service is in four sentences; what you are asking for.

Page two: first-year cost and funding mix; the three objections answered; one comparable service elsewhere in Europe; the decision you need.

Make the ask small. Request a feasibility mandate and a working group, not a budget line. A yes to something small is worth more than a maybe to something large, and it puts you into Phase 2 with authority.

What others actually didTwo of the INNOVATE guide's fifteen recommendations belong on your page two, verbatim: “be aware that it might take a lot of time before seeing first results”, and “think big, start small: prove concept and scale”. A small ask is the credible ask.Attributed to the INNOVATE guidebook (Energy Cities, 2020) and EU Peers Deliverable 4.4 (2026).

  • Have you removed every sentence that only you find interesting?

Define the territory you will serve, then understand the buildings and the households inside it.

Start hereAsk your planning department for housing stock by age and tenure for one candidate area. One email, one attachment, and you can start.
Why this mattersTerritory size decides whether the numbers can ever work. Get it wrong and three years later the review concludes that the concept failed, when what failed was the catchment.
Hand off or borrowPlanning can produce most of this. You decide which segment comes first.
The realityIf the administrative area and the actual market do not match, say so in writing early. It is much harder to say later.
How wide do you draw the boundary?
One municipality
Simple governance and one budget holder. Renovation volume may be too thin to sustain a team.
A group of municipalities
Enough volume, shared cost. Slower decisions and a governance structure to build. Seven Dutch municipalities pooled €1.2m over five years this way.
Regional
Volume and funding access. You lose the local trust that converts interest into signed work.

What you have when it is doneA one-page profile: building stock, tenure split, household segments, estimated annual renovation volume.

Guidance, checkpoints and resources

Decide the level first: a municipality, a group of municipalities, a region. Then characterise what is inside it — building types and age, tenure (owner-occupied, private rented, social, condominium), energy performance where data exists, and a realistic annual renovation volume.

Profile the households as well as the buildings: income bands, tenure, age, household composition. Then ask what they actually want. Comfort, a warmer bedroom, a new bathroom, lower bills — in roughly that order. Renovation demand is rarely about energy.

Be specific about which segment you will serve first. Serving everybody from day one is the most common way to serve nobody well.

What others actually didReimarkt chose its service area by the presence of qualified suppliers, not by homeowner demand alone. The Riga Energy Agency notes that smaller cities may lack local suppliers entirely, and that renovation potential has to be evaluated first. Heerlen's market analysis found a market too dispersed, with little or no connection between supply and demand.Attributed to the INNOVATE guidebook (Energy Cities, 2020) and EU Peers Deliverable 4.4 (2026).
Checkpoint questions
  • Is the annual renovation volume large enough to sustain a team?
  • Which building type and tenure will you serve first, and why that one?
  • Can you name the three household segments you are designing for?
 
Common mistakeLetting the boundary be drawn by whoever's budget is paying, rather than by where the buildings are.

Find out what actually stops households renovating in your area — not what stops them in general.

Start hereBook three twenty-minute calls this week: your housing officer, one condominium manager, one installer. Ask each the same question — why do people here not renovate?
Why this mattersA service is a set of answers to barriers. Answer the wrong ones and everything downstream is well-built and beside the point.
Hand off or borrowShare the calls with a colleague, but be on at least four yourself. The value is in hearing it, not reading it.
The realityYou do not need a consultant or a survey for this. Ten conversations are enough to rank three barriers, and they cost nothing but time.

What you have when it is doneThree ranked local barriers, with the evidence behind the ranking.

Guidance, checkpoints and resources

The usual candidates: low awareness, upfront cost, the split incentive between landlord and tenant, distrust of contractors, confusion about subsidies, fear of disruption, and decision paralysis in condominiums where everyone must agree.

Rank them for your area, not from the literature. Ten conversations with households will teach you more than any report. Speak to three contractors too — they know exactly why quotes do not convert, and nobody asks them.

Separate what a service can remove from what is structural. Being clear about the second protects you from promising it.

What others actually didFrederikshavn in Denmark found its messages “focused too much on the potential financial savings, which led to misunderstanding — homeowners expected a quick payback”. The same municipality reports it may take up to two years before homeowners become interested at all. Brussels named lack of trust between all parties as its major barrier.Attributed to the INNOVATE guidebook (Energy Cities, 2020) and EU Peers Deliverable 4.4 (2026).
Checkpoint questions
  • Which three barriers dominate locally, and what is your evidence?
  • Which of them can a service realistically remove?
  • Which are structural and outside your control — and have you said so out loud?
 
Common mistakeCommissioning a study instead of making ten telephone calls.

Map the local and regional commitments, schemes and campaigns your service can plug into.

Start hereTelephone your regional energy agency and ask what renovation funding exists and when it expires. They keep this list; you do not have to build it.
Why this mattersPolitical cover is the cheapest resource available to you, and it is already sitting in documents your authority has signed.
Hand off or borrowYour regional energy agency will hand you most of this if you ask.
The realityNote what is time-limited. Funding that expires inside your set-up period is not funding — it is a deadline.

What you have when it is doneA list of policies, targets and schemes you can attach to, with the administering body named for each.

Guidance, checkpoints and resources

Look for four things: climate and energy plans with renovation rates attached; financial support schemes aimed at specific groups; measures aimed at the supply side, such as contractor training or certification; and awareness campaigns already funded.

For each one, note who administers it. Those people are your future partners — or your future competitors for the same budget line. Better to know which now.

Check the regional layer as well as the local one. Renovation money frequently sits one level above where the service will operate.

What others actually didParity Projects began with the measures that attracted the national grant scheme, which meant the funding argument was already made.   • Is any of this money time-limited, and when does it expire?

List everyone already active in renovation in your area, what each offers, and where the gaps are.

Start hereFind the person who has worked in your local renovation market for ten years and buy them a coffee. Bring a blank sheet, not a template.
Why this mattersYou will either work with these organisations or against them. The second is far more expensive, and you rarely get to choose once positions have hardened.
Hand off or borrowSomebody who has worked in the local renovation market for ten years will give you most of this over one coffee. Find that person first.
The realityCoordination across many actors was named in the INNOVATE guide as time-consuming and as a genuine disadvantage of the coordinating model. Budget attention for it, not just goodwill.

What you have when it is doneAn actor map: offer, reach, gap — plus a named contact in each organisation that matters.

Guidance, checkpoints and resources

Cover the whole chain: installers and suppliers, architects and energy auditors, existing advice services, banks and credit unions, housing associations, condominium managers, social services, consumer organisations, and any service already calling itself a one-stop shop.

For each, record three things — what they offer, who they reach, what they are missing. The gaps in that table are the beginnings of your value proposition.

Map individuals, not only organisations. Partnerships are agreed by people, and one enthusiastic manager is worth more than a signed memorandum with an institution.

Turn the map into working relationships before you need them.

Start herePick one organisation that already reaches your target households and do them one small unpaid favour this month. Ask for nothing.
Why this mattersReferrals from organisations households already trust are the cheapest route to your first users. Everything else costs money you will not have.
Hand off or borrowNo — these relationships are personal, and they follow you rather than your post.
The realityStart it now, finish it later. This is the one Phase 2 step that legitimately runs in parallel with the design work.

What you have when it is doneThree named partner organisations with a stated mutual interest, and one peer service you can call.

Guidance, checkpoints and resources

Start with the organisations that already reach your target households — social services, condominium managers, housing associations, community groups. Offer something before you ask for something.

Then look upward and outward. Links with regional and national initiatives strengthen your position, and peers running the same service elsewhere will save you from mistakes that would otherwise cost you a year.

Test partnerships with a favour before formalising them with an agreement. Enthusiasm that survives a small unpaid task is real; enthusiasm in a meeting is not yet evidence.

Decide what your service is for — and what you will refuse to do.

Start herePut one hour on your steering group's next agenda under the heading “what is this service for”. Do not bring a draft mission — bring the trade-offs.
Why this mattersA vague purpose is what allows every new funder, every new director and every enthusiastic colleague to reopen the question. Written down and agreed, it is a door you can close.
Hand off or borrowThe drafting, no. The meeting, insist on it.
The realityD4.4 asks you to be able to explain the purpose in one or two sentences. If you cannot, the group has not actually agreed.

What you have when it is doneOne mission sentence, three numbered goals with dates, two stated exclusions.

Guidance, checkpoints and resources

Settle the trade-offs explicitly. How much weight goes to deep renovation against quick wins? To sustainable materials against lowest cost? To reaching low-income households who cannot pay against volume that keeps you solvent? These pull against each other, and pretending otherwise postpones the argument to a worse moment.

Write the mission in one sentence. Then three goals beneath it, each with a number and a date.

Then write down two things you will not do. A mission with no exclusions is decoration.

What others actually didThe INNOVATE guide treats “deep renovation or step-by-step approach?” as an open strategic choice with both answers viable — which means it is yours to make and to write down. Reimarkt resolved it by standardising products for defined segments rather than tailoring each project.Attributed to the INNOVATE guidebook (Energy Cities, 2020) and EU Peers Deliverable 4.4 (2026).
Checkpoint questions
  • If a funder offered money for something outside your mission, would your team know to decline?
  • Can you state the mission without the words “sustainable”, “holistic” or “integrated”?
  • Could any of your three goals plausibly fail? If not, they are not goals.
 
Common mistakeWriting it alone. If your steering group did not write it with you, it will be rewritten without you.

Decide what you offer, to whom, and why anyone would choose you over doing nothing.

Start hereAsk two contractors what would make them work through you rather than direct. Their answer is half your proposition and you cannot guess it.
Why this mattersThe offer has to work twice — for households and for the installers you cannot operate without. Most designs are tested only on the first group.
Hand off or borrowA value proposition canvas session runs well with an external facilitator if you have one. The content is yours.
The realityChanging contractors' and suppliers' minds was named by one INNOVATE service as its main challenge. If installers see you as an extra step between them and the customer, they will not take part — and without them there is no service.
How much of the chain do you carry?
Advice only
Cheap, fast to launch, and the households most in need will still not act.
Advice plus coordination
The common middle. You broker contractors without holding the contract, so liability stays with them.
End-to-end, including works
Highest conversion, highest liability, and you need capital. Few services in Europe reach this.
The tool for this step
Value Proposition Canvas
Start here. Maps what households are trying to get done, what gets in the way, and what your service removes.
Business Model Canvas
Then this. Nine blocks — the customer, the offer, the channels, the costs, the revenue. Fill it badly first, then again properly.

What you have when it is doneA completed value proposition canvas, plus a service list split into deliver, broker and decline.

Guidance, checkpoints and resources

Start with the questions: who are your customers, who are your stakeholders, what problem do you remove, and what do you offer that removes it? Energy audits, tailored advice, subsidy applications, financing support, contractor selection and coordination, supervision of works, checks afterwards.

Then sort every service into three columns: deliver yourself, broker from someone else, decline. The third column is the one that makes the offer legible.

A value proposition canvas is the right tool here, and the business model canvas follows naturally from it.

Map every step from a household's first thought to the finished works, and find where they drop out.

Start hereFind one household that renovated recently and walk them backwards through it, stage by stage, writing down every point they nearly stopped.
Why this mattersThe losses along the journey are far larger than anyone expects. One service that tracked it properly found that of the households it reached, 10% made contact, 1.5% asked for an offer and 0.38% went ahead — over an average journey of eleven months. Design for those numbers or design for disappointment.
Hand off or borrowDo it alongside whoever will actually answer the telephone. Not instead of them.
The realityOne journey per segment, and condominiums are their own case. In one pilot, decision-making across several years meant three buildings completed where fifteen had been planned.
Where does the online offer stop?
Online throughout, human on request
Scales cheaply. Loses the older and less confident households, who are often the ones in cold homes.
Online funnel, human from the assessment on
The pattern most services converge on. Portal reduces early enquiries; a person carries the decision.
Human throughout
Highest trust, highest cost per household, and it does not scale past your headcount.

What you have when it is doneOne journey map per segment, with drop-out points marked and a named owner for each handover.

Guidance, checkpoints and resources

Build one journey per segment. A condominium behaves nothing like a single-family owner: the decision is collective, slower, and needs a different entry point entirely.

For each stage — awareness, first contact, assessment, offer, decision, financing, works, completion, follow-up — record what the household does, what you do, and what could stop them.

Pay particular attention to handovers: online to offline, you to contractor, advice to finance. Drop-out concentrates there. Then decide deliberately where the online offer stops and a person takes over.

What others actually didReimarkt tracked its own funnel and reports conversion of between 0.5% and 10%, over a journey of at least eleven months. Parity Projects calls it “the funnel of death”: if many clients drop out before works start, the cost per completed job rises radically. Pass Rénovation in France finds 35% of contacts arrive from the internet, 20% by word of mouth and 15% from the press.Attributed to the INNOVATE guidebook (Energy Cities, 2020) and EU Peers Deliverable 4.4 (2026).

  • How many times must a household repeat their story to a different person?

Model three years of money, then run it again at half the volume.

Start hereWrite to two comparable services and ask for their real cost and revenue figures. Most share them. Modelling from a template produces numbers nobody believes.
Why this mattersThis is the step that decides whether the service exists, and the one most often written in a weekend because a funder asked.
Hand off or borrowAsk two comparable services for their real figures. Modelling from templates produces numbers nobody believes.
The realityHomeowner interest itself can take up to two years to build in a new area. That lag belongs in the model, not in a footnote.
Where does the money come from after year one?
Household fees as a percentage of works
Predictable and it scales with volume. One Danish service charges 5% for single-family homes and 10–15% for condominiums.
A flat fee per project
Easier to explain and to budget. One French service charges about €1,550 per single-family project.
Public funding, renewed annually
Most common in practice. It exposes you to the annual budget round and to elections.
The tool for this step
Business Model Canvas
Use the cost structure and revenue streams blocks as your checklist. If a line has no home on the canvas, question whether it belongs in the model.

What you have when it is doneA three-year cost and revenue model, a half-volume scenario, and a cost-per-completed-renovation figure.

Guidance, checkpoints and resources

Costs: adviser and project manager salaries, marketing staff, IT infrastructure (CRM, digital platforms, calculators), communications and outreach, premises, travel, overheads.

Revenues: household service fees, fixed or as a percentage of works value; commissions from partner contractors or suppliers; public grants and programme funding; fees for services delivered to municipalities.

Model three years with explicit volume assumptions. Then halve the volume and look again. The second model is the one that tells you whether the design survives contact with reality — and it is the one your finance officer will ask for.

Work out what a single completed renovation costs you to deliver. Almost nobody knows this number, and it governs everything.

Pass Rénovation charges about €1,550 per single-family project. Reimarkt: €120,000 to start, €86,000 a year to run. Brussels: €500,000 a year for sixteen people.Attributed to the INNOVATE guidebook (Energy Cities, 2020) and EU Peers Deliverable 4.4 (2026).
Checkpoint questions
  • What proportion of year-three income is public money?
  • Can you survive a year at half your projected volume?
  • What does one completed renovation cost you, end to end?
 
Common mistakeModelling break-even inside two years. Real services report two to three years to break even and five to eight years to a viable model. Your finance officer will believe the rest of your document if this number is honest.

Choose the legal form, and settle liability, data protection and contracts before you trade.

Start hereSend your legal service three questions this week: who is liable for the works, what is our lawful basis for household data, and does state aid apply.
Why this mattersLiability decides whether good contractors will join you and whether a household will act on your recommendation. It is a design constraint, not paperwork.
Hand off or borrowYes — legal and your data protection officer do this. Your job is to ask them the three questions below and get the answers in writing.
The realityTwo that catch public bodies specifically: where the service competes with private providers, EU state aid rules apply; and dependence on annual budget negotiation limits what you can contractually commit to.
Which legal form?
In-house (public)
No state aid exposure, no procurement of a partner. Nobody can be dedicated to it full-time and it moves at municipal speed.
Procured lead partner
One Danish municipality made a one-time payment to a procured energy consultancy, which then earned homeowner fees. Keeps you out of daily operations.
Private company or cooperative
Agile and able to sell. State aid rules bite where you compete with private providers, and you will need local authority backing anyway.
Public-private partnership
Splits liability and capital. You give up direct control, which is harder to explain to a council than it sounds.

What you have when it is doneChosen legal form, a data protection register, draft household and contractor agreements, a written complaints process.

Guidance, checkpoints and resources

Choose the legal entity — private, public, or a public-private partnership — to fit the services you deliver and the money you take. The form follows the function, not the other way round.

Then settle four things. Data protection: a lawful basis for the household data you hold, and compliance for every digital tool you use. Liability: who is answerable for the quality of works, for advice given, for financing outcomes. Contracts: standard household agreements covering scope, guarantees and payment terms, plus agreements with contractors and lenders. Redress: a process for household complaints, contractor disputes and compliance checks.

Write the complaints process before you need it. Writing it during your first dispute is how services lose their reputation in a fortnight.

What others actually didFrederikshavn kept the municipality out of operations: a one-time grant to a publicly procured lead partner, which then earns homeowner fees. Reimarkt operates as a private company. Brussels chose an iterative route, testing methodology and tools on four condominiums before committing to a form.Attributed to the INNOVATE guidebook (Energy Cities, 2020) and EU Peers Deliverable 4.4 (2026).

  • If a renovation goes wrong, who does the household hold responsible?
  • Do you have a lawful basis, in writing, for every category of household data you hold?
  • Can a contractor be removed from your network, on what grounds, and who decides?
 
Common mistakeTreating state aid as a question for later. It shapes which model is available to you.

Decide the governance, the roles and the competencies — then plan how people keep learning.

Start hereWrite the two competencies you will not compromise on before you write the job description. One of them should not be technical.
Why this mattersYou will not be able to dedicate anyone to this exclusively. The INNOVATE guide lists exactly that as a disadvantage of running it in-house — staff carry other duties in parallel. Plan for split attention rather than pretending it away.
Hand off or borrowHR runs the process. You define the two competencies that matter and refuse to compromise on the second one.
The realityTechnical knowledge can be trained. Patience with an anxious householder mostly cannot. Recruit for the second and train the first.

What you have when it is doneAn org chart, role descriptions, and a first-year training plan with a budget line.

Guidance, checkpoints and resources

A small service typically needs a coordinator, one or two technical advisers, someone who can walk a household through paperwork without condescension, and communications capacity. Shared or part-time roles are normal at the start.

Settle governance at the same time: who decides, who oversees, who reports to whom, and who decides when the coordinator is away.

Plan continuing development from the beginning. Regulation, technologies and subsidy schemes all move faster than any induction can cover. Put a training budget per person in the first-year plan, before someone else spends it.

What others actually didBrussels staffs architects, engineers and energy experts alongside social mediators — and describes the mediators as very important in triggering renovations at all. Heerlen ran with one director and two people covering sales, advice and technical work. Frederikshavn keeps a municipal project manager responsible for supervising and monitoring the works.Attributed to the INNOVATE guidebook (Energy Cities, 2020) and EU Peers Deliverable 4.4 (2026).
Checkpoint questions
  • Who makes decisions when the coordinator is on leave?
  • Which competency will you struggle hardest to recruit, and what is your fallback?
  • What is your training budget per person per year?
 
Common mistakeBuilding a rota that assumes full-time people you will not get.

Turn Phase 2's contacts into working agreements with lenders, municipalities and contractors.

Start hereInvite three contractors you respect to help you set the entry criteria for your network. They will be harder on quality than you would be.
Why this mattersA household judges the whole service by the contractor who turns up. That relationship is the product, whatever the strategy document says.
Hand off or borrowNo, but a respected local contractor helping you set the entry criteria is worth more than any procurement template.
The realityContractors are the slowest group to move, and the guides are consistent about it. Start in Phase 3 if you can.
Local contractors or larger firms?
Local only
Political appeal and local economic benefit. Smaller firms may not be able to meet your quality criteria — the INNOVATE guide says so plainly.
Whoever meets the criteria
One Dutch service began with five local suppliers and ended up working with national companies that could meet its standards.
A mixed panel with published criteria
Slower to assemble, defensible in a council meeting, and it lets you remove a firm without removing the whole model.

What you have when it is doneSigned or drafted agreements with at least one lender, one municipal department and a founding contractor group.

Guidance, checkpoints and resources

Work with banks on green loan products your households can actually access — a product designed for a different income profile is worse than no product, because it produces a refusal you get blamed for.

Partner with municipalities for grant facilitation and for referrals, which are the cheapest households you will ever acquire.

Build a contractor and supplier network with explicit entry criteria, stated quality expectations, and a way out. Decide early whether you recommend one contractor or present several — it changes your liability, your credibility and your workload.

What others actually didReimarkt “started to work with five local suppliers but ended up working with bigger companies at national level that were able to meet our quality criteria” — and INNOVATE lists “local suppliers might not be able to meet your quality criteria” as one of its fifteen recommendations. Heerlen negotiated directly with manufacturers, buying around a thousand heat pumps at a reduced price.Attributed to the INNOVATE guidebook (Energy Cities, 2020) and EU Peers Deliverable 4.4 (2026).
Checkpoint questions
  • What are the entry criteria for your contractor network, and who applies them?
  • What happens when a contractor underperforms — and has anyone written that down?
  • Does your lending partner's product fit the income profile of the households you serve?
 
Common mistakeRecruiting contractors on enthusiasm. The most available ones are usually available for a reason.

Work out what actually motivates each segment, then choose messages, channels and messengers to match.

Start hereList your target segments and, beside each, the person they already trust on housing. That column, not your logo, is your channel plan.
Why this mattersHouseholds renovate for a warm room, a dry wall, a new kitchen. Leading with energy savings speaks only to the small group who were coming anyway.
Hand off or borrowBring in the expertise. The INNOVATE guide is blunt that authorities do not always have marketing and communication people in-house — and this is the function where the gap shows fastest.
The realityThe messenger usually matters more than the message. A neighbour who has done it will outperform your logo for the first two years.
Who carries the message?
Your own brand and channels
Full control, and for the first two years almost no recognition.
Trusted intermediaries
Neighbours, community organisations, contractors. Slower to set up, far higher conversion.
Bought marketing capability
Recommendation nine of the INNOVATE guide is to get real marketers on board. Most authorities do not have this in-house.

What you have when it is doneA communication plan by segment: motivation, message, channel, messenger — covering the whole journey.

Guidance, checkpoints and resources

Start from motivation, segment by segment, and match the message to it. The energy saving is a supporting argument, not the opening one.

Then choose the messenger, which usually matters more than the message. A neighbour who has done it, a trusted local institution, a contractor with a reputation — all outperform your own brand in your first two years, when you have no brand.

Choose channels where your segment already is, not where communication is easiest to produce. And build the plan across the entire customer journey: most services plan the top of the funnel carefully and go silent the moment someone says yes.

What others actually didFrederikshavn ran comprehensive campaigns across all local media, the municipal website, Facebook and face-to-face meetings — and separately learned that leading on financial savings misled people. Pass Rénovation knows exactly where its contacts come from: 35% internet, 20% word of mouth, 15% press. INNOVATE's ninth recommendation is blunt: get real marketers and salesmen on board.Attributed to the INNOVATE guidebook (Energy Cities, 2020) and EU Peers Deliverable 4.4 (2026).

Reach the households least likely ever to reach you.

Start hereRing your social services colleagues before anyone in the energy sector. Ask which households they already visit who live in cold homes.
Why this mattersThe households with most to gain are the least likely to come to you. They arrive through people they already trust, or not at all.
Hand off or borrowSocial services already hold these relationships. Start there rather than in the energy sector.
The realityIf your only route is a loan, you have designed a service for households who needed less help. Have one offer that works without borrowing.

What you have when it is doneTwo named community partners, a two-way referral route, and one offer designed for households who cannot borrow.

Guidance, checkpoints and resources

Local NGOs, energy agencies, community groups, social services, tenant associations and faith organisations hold relationships you cannot build quickly and credibility you cannot buy.

Offer something concrete rather than a partnership in principle: training for their front-line staff, a named contact who answers the phone, a referral route that works in both directions.

Design for the barriers a standard service does not remove — no capital, no access to credit, rented homes where the landlord decides, and a well-earned distrust of officialdom. If your only route is a loan, you have designed a service for people who did not need much help.

What others actually didPass Rénovation covers the full cost of works, pays suppliers directly, and lets the household repay over 15 to 25 years at 2.5% — which reaches people who cannot borrow commercially. Frederikshavn negotiated bank terms explicitly covering low-income households. Brussels employs social mediators for the same reason.Attributed to the INNOVATE guidebook (Energy Cities, 2020) and EU Peers Deliverable 4.4 (2026).

  • Which organisation already sits with your hardest-to-reach households?
  • What does your service offer somebody who cannot borrow?
  • Have you designed anything specifically for tenants, or only for owners?

Give people somewhere to walk into and a person to speak to.

Start hereBefore you look at premises, arrange for a telephone line that a person answers. It matters more on day one than a counter does.
Why this mattersTrust is built in person, particularly for older householders about to spend a large part of their savings on a stranger's advice.
Hand off or borrowEstates. Your input is location and opening hours that suit people who work.
The realityA telephone answered by a person matters more on day one than a counter does. If premises are slow, open anyway.

What you have when it is doneA premises decision, a service channel plan, and opening hours that suit working households.

Guidance, checkpoints and resources

Find premises that are genuinely easy to reach — centrally located, on a transport route, ideally somewhere people already go for another reason.

Set up the service channels deliberately: an in-person counter, a place to sit down and work through options without being hurried, and a phone line answered by a human being.

Some services go further, with mobile offices, market stalls or pop-ups in neighbourhoods where a renovation programme is running. Going to people costs less than persuading them to come to you.

Buy, copy, or build?
Off-the-shelf CRM
Live in weeks, fits imperfectly, and the supplier maintains it.
Copy a peer service's setup
Cheapest good answer. Several services will show you theirs, and their configuration already reflects this sector.

Open small, learn fast from the first twenty households, then make noise.

Start hereChoose one neighbourhood and twenty households, and write down the five numbers you will watch. Then say out loud, in a meeting, that twenty is the target.
Why this mattersA launch is a rehearsal followed by a moment. Advertising before the workflow has survived real households means spending your credibility on finding your own faults.
Hand off or borrowThe monitoring, yes. Choosing the five numbers, no.
The realityPlan the small version and expect it to be smaller still. One pilot designed for fifteen condominiums recruited four. That is not failure, it is the base rate.

What you have when it is doneA test phase plan, a launch communication plan, and a five-number monitoring dashboard with a named owner.

Guidance, checkpoints and resources

Run a test phase with a limited group — one neighbourhood, one building type, twenty households. The purpose is to find where your workflows break, not to hit a target. Say so internally, or somebody will report it as a target.

Then focus communication on making the offer known and generating interest, using the plan from step 4.3.

From the first day, measure: enquiries, conversion to assessment, assessment to signed works, works completed, satisfaction — and which channel produced each. Evaluate channel effectiveness early enough that you can still move the budget.

What others actually didBrussels started with a pilot of four condominiums, expressly to test the methodology and the tools. Reimarkt names its two mistakes in order: “one of the first mistakes was to establish the shop before the products”, and “the second biggest mistake of Reimarkt was to upscale to five shops at once. Looking back this was too soon.”Attributed to the INNOVATE guidebook (Energy Cities, 2020) and EU Peers Deliverable 4.4 (2026).
